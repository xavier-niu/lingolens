The Art of Slow Travel

Slow travel is less about where you go and more about how you go there. Instead of rushing between famous sights, you settle into one place for a while and let it reveal itself at its own pace.

When you stay longer, you start to notice the small things: the baker who remembers your order, the way the light changes on the old stone walls, the rhythm of the market on a Saturday morning.

Why it matters

Travelling slowly also tends to be kinder to the places you visit. You spend money in local shops, you take trains instead of short flights, and you leave room for conversations that would never happen on a packed itinerary.

It is not for everyone, and it does take time. But if you can manage it, even once, you may find that you come home with fewer photos and far better stories.
